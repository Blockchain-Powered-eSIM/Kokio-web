Kokio never asks who you are. There's no signup form, no email field and no passport upload. You open the app, create a passkey with Face ID or your fingerprint, and buy a plan.

## What Kokio leaves out

- **No ID check.** Every plan in the catalogue is marked as needing none.
- **No account record with your name or email.** Your account is a smart contract wallet tied to a passkey, and the passkey never leaves your phone's secure hardware.
- **No password**, so there's nothing to phish or reuse.

What we never collect, we can't leak. There's no name, email or passport on file for a breach to expose.

## What no-KYC does not cover

No-KYC is not anonymity. Once you're connected, the network still sees your device identifier and still logs an IP address. An eSIM adds no encryption either, so if you need that, run a VPN on top.

How you pay leaves its own trail. Card, Apple Pay and Google Pay go through Stripe. If you'd rather keep a card out of it, you can [pay in crypto](/buy-esim-with-crypto).

Our post on [all types of no-KYC eSIMs](/blogs/all-kinds-of-no-kyc-esims) goes through each layer and what every kind of provider does and doesn't protect.

## Why skip the local SIM

Many countries tie a local SIM to your passport. In Thailand, operators have had to check a passport for every SIM sold to a foreign visitor since 16 May 2026 ([Yozzo](https://www.yozzo.com/mvno-news/thailand-nbtc-sim-card-registration-kyc-measures/), [Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thailand-tightens-telecom-registration-data-and-ip-address-rules-to-combat-tech-crime/)). A Kokio plan connects by roaming, so there is no local SIM to register.
