Buying a SIM abroad usually starts with a passport scan, a name and an email. That data doesn't stay with one company. It gets copied to carriers, resellers and payment processors, and telecom is one of the most-breached industries there is. Our post on [where your SIM data goes](/blogs/where-your-sim-data-goes) traces the whole path.

A data-only eSIM has no technical need to know who you are, so Kokio doesn't ask.

## What Kokio keeps

Nothing that identifies you. You sign in with a passkey created with Face ID or your fingerprint, and it never leaves your phone's secure hardware. There's no password to steal, and no record of your name or email to sell, subpoena or leak.

## What still sees you

The mobile network still sees your device and logs an IP address while you're connected, and your payment method keeps its own records. Card payments go through Stripe. If you'd rather not use a card, [pay in crypto](/buy-esim-with-crypto). A VPN covers your traffic, which no eSIM does on its own.

## Why it's built this way

We don't think you should have to trust a provider with your identity to get online. So the account is a smart contract wallet, built on an [open source contract suite](https://github.com/Blockchain-Powered-eSIM/smart-contract-suite) audited by Ethereum Foundation PSE and by CD Security. You can read the code instead of reading our promises.

The [manifesto](/manifesto) sets out the reasoning. For the short version of what we skip at checkout, see [no-KYC eSIM](/no-kyc-esim).
